01Why this quarter

Two clocks are running at once.

Neither is a marketing trend. One is federal policy. The other is your own acquisition schedule.

Clock one · Coverage

Close to nine million Canadians are now covered by the CDCP.

The latest expansion made up to 4.5 million adults aged eighteen to sixty-four eligible. Many haven’t seen a dentist in years. They’re picking one now, and they pick the way people pick a dentist in a hurry: search, scan the reviews, call whoever answers. That patient then comes back every six months for a decade. You built a CDCP hub and city locator pages, so none of this is news. The part worth saying out loud is that it only happens once. Each of those patients gets chosen by somebody.

Clock two · Integration

Every clinic you buy arrives with someone else’s marketing.

A different agency. A different Google Ads account, built by someone you’ll never meet. Different tracking, or none. A different idea of what counts as a new patient. Thirty-five times last year, plus twenty-seven more from a single group. Every one of those clinics is a number your team can’t compare to the clinic beside it. Comparability is the whole reason a network is worth more than the sum of its practices.

02What breaks at five hundred

Local marketing doesn’t fail at scale. It fragments.

None of this is a criticism of how 123Dentist runs marketing. It’s the arithmetic of buying five hundred independent practices that each kept their own name, their own site and their own vendor.

One

Five hundred definitions of a new patient.

One agency counts form fills. Another counts phone calls, including the ones nobody picked up. A third counts anyone who clicked. Add those together and you get a network number nobody can defend line by line. That’s the only kind of number that survives a board.

Two

The best clinic and the worst clinic look identical from head office.

Without a common measure, a clinic with a marketing problem looks the same as a clinic with a front-desk problem, or a clinic in a slower market. So budget becomes a negotiation instead of a calculation. The loudest region wins.

Three

Onboarding a clinic takes longer than buying it.

The deal closes. Then someone has to track down the old agency, recover the ad account, find out who owns the Business Profile, discover tracking was never installed, and rebuild all of it. Thirty-five times a year. Meanwhile the campaigns either run unwatched or stop.

Four

Vendor count grows faster than clinic count.

Every acquisition can bring a contract, an invoice, a login and a quarterly call. You chose none of them. They all end up on your desk.

A better agency doesn’t fix this. A standard unit does: one package, built the same way every time, producing one number that means the same thing at every location. That’s the proposal.

04How a booked patient is measured

Three layers. Two are ours. One is yours.

Layer one
Ours.

A tracked number per clinic, inserted dynamically on your existing site. Which call came from which keyword, at what hour, in which city. Your site. Your brand. Our tracking.

Layer two
Ours.

The booking path on the site, tracked end to end, including where people drop out.

Layer three
Yours.

Whether the call became an appointment, and whether the patient showed. That lives in your practice management system, and it stays there.

We do not integrate with Dentrix, Tracker, Cleardent, or any other practice management system. No integration. No IT project. No patient data leaves the clinic.

We need six columns in a shared sheet: date, tracked number, answered, booked, appointment date, showed. We fill the left half automatically. The front desk fills three columns. Two minutes a week.

Where a clinic can’t complete the reconciliation, that clinic’s dashboard reads “qualified calls,” not “booked patients.” We’d rather under-claim than get caught over-claiming.

The moment a marketing vendor asks for practice-management access, it stops being your decision. It becomes an IT decision, a privacy decision and a legal decision. That review takes months. It’s why most attribution projects inside dental groups are still unfinished. Six columns in a spreadsheet isn’t a compromise. It’s the version that ships in fourteen days.

Said plainly

We have no dental case study at network scale. That’s why this asks for six clinics and not five hundred. It’s why the thresholds are published before launch instead of picked afterward, and why the fee cancels monthly with nothing to claw back. We’re not asking you to believe a claim. We’re offering the cheapest way to test one. If the number holds, the data is yours and it came from your clinics. If it doesn’t, you’re out four months of a cancelable fee and you know exactly where your Google funnel breaks. That’s worth knowing either way.

The arithmetic, stated once

At the floor — eight booked new patients per clinic per month — six clinics produce 576 patients in a year. Five hundred produce roughly forty-eight thousand. We’re not promising forty-eight thousand patients. We’re showing the shape of the unit if it repeats. What one new patient is worth to 123Dentist is a number you have and we don’t. We give you the cost side and fix the threshold in advance. The multiplication is yours.

08Where we fit

This replaces nothing you already have.

A proposal that quietly displaces an internal team or an existing vendor dies in a hallway. So here’s the boundary, drawn on purpose.

We run
  • Google Search, Display and Video for each pilot clinic
  • The Business Profile for each pilot clinic — accuracy, categories, photos, review replies
  • Call tracking, conversion tracking and the per-clinic dashboard
  • Review capture at the front desk
We don’t touch
  • Your brand system, your creative standards or your national campaigns
  • Your websites — one snippet and one number, nothing else changes
  • Your practice management system, and no patient data at all
  • Your internal marketing team’s remit — we report to it, not around it
On call intelligence

If you run Patient Prism or anything like it, keep it. Prism grades the conversation. We buy the conversation. Different jobs, and the two datasets reconcile. Audit our booked-patient number against Prism’s call outcomes. We’d rather you did. Judge us on your instrument, not ours.

On incumbent agencies

Nothing needs cancelling to run this. Pick six clinics where the incumbent is underperforming, or six with no agency at all, or run both in one market and compare. We’re not asking you to break a relationship to test a hypothesis.

10What comes after Google

You own five hundred clinics. Your patients have never heard of you.

Your clinics kept their own names. 12th & 8th Dental, 404 Dental, 7 Oaks. Right call for the practices, and it leaves you with a problem no advertising budget solves: at the point of care, the network is invisible. Google stops at the front door. These layers start there. Neither is sold or priced in this document. They’re the reason a four-month pilot is worth starting as a relationship.

Layer one · in market

The front desk pad, co-branded.

The NFC review pad is already in the package. The network version carries both marks: the clinic name the patient knows, and 123Dentist under it. They tap it when they’re happiest, leave a Google review that lowers the cost of the next patient in that city, and find out the practice they trust is part of something bigger. One object doing local SEO and network brand at once.

Layer two · in build

The waiting room, and the recall that never gets made.

A Wi-Fi sign-in screen carrying the network’s brand, collecting contact details with consent from every patient who connects. Branded chargers and hardware that put 123Dentist in the room instead of on a contract. And an AI recall agent working the list every clinic has and no front desk has time for: patients overdue for hygiene, treatment plans accepted and never booked. That list is already sitting in your network. Nobody is calling it.

None of this is in the pilot, the fee, or what we’re asking for today. It’s here so the four-month question gets answered with the whole picture.

12Questions you’ll be asked

Answered here, so you’re not defending them from memory.

What does this replace?

Nothing. Not your team, not your national campaigns, not your website vendor, not your call intelligence platform, not an agency you’d rather keep. It’s a per-clinic execution layer that sits underneath what you already run.

Who owns the accounts and the data?

123Dentist, from day one, without exception. Ads accounts live in your manager account, Business Profiles stay yours, and every month of data exports as files on request. We hold access, never ownership.

Do you need access to our practice management system?

No. No integration, no IT project, no patient data. The reconciliation is six columns in a shared sheet, three of them filled by the front desk, two minutes a week.

How do three people serve a network this size?

The build is a written spec, not a bespoke project, and execution runs on fulfilment infrastructure already handling several hundred accounts to that spec. Strategy, thresholds and accountability stay with the named partners. Build time doesn’t change with volume.

What happens when a clinic underperforms?

It appears by name on the roll-up, ranked last, with the reason on the same page. Usually answer rate, sometimes a genuinely thinner market. We tell you before you ask. Where a clinic can’t reconcile its bookings we report qualified calls and claim nothing further.

Why not give this to an agency we already use?

You can, and for one clinic it barely matters. We’re not claiming better campaigns. We’re claiming that five hundred clinics built to one spec produce one comparable number, and a roster of agencies inherited through acquisition never will.

What’s the real risk here?

That four months pass, the number lands above your threshold, and it cost you four months of a cancelable per-clinic fee plus a media budget you controlled throughout. No term, no exit fee, nothing to recover. What’s left is a precise read on where your Google funnel breaks, useful to whoever runs it next.

Why does this need to happen this quarter?

Millions of newly covered adults are picking a dentist right now, once. And every month of delay is another batch of acquired clinics onboarded onto measurement you’ll have to redo. Neither clock waits for a budget cycle.
