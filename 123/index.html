01Why this quarter

Two clocks are running at once.

Neither of them is a marketing trend. One is federal policy. The other is your own acquisition schedule.

Clock one · Coverage

Close to nine million Canadians are now covered by the CDCP.

Up to 4.5 million adults aged eighteen to sixty-four became eligible in the latest expansion. A large share of them have not had a regular dentist in years. They are choosing one now, and they choose the way everyone chooses a dentist under time pressure — they search, they read the reviews, they call the first clinic that answers. A patient captured in this window recalls for a decade. You built a CDCP hub and city locator pages, so this is not news to you. What is worth saying out loud is that the window closes: these patients get chosen once.

Clock two · Integration

Every clinic you buy arrives with someone else’s marketing.

A different agency. A different Google Ads account, structured by someone you will never meet. A different tracking setup, or none. A different idea of what counts as a new patient. Thirty-five times last year, plus twenty-seven more from a single group. Every one of those clinics is a number your team cannot compare to the clinic beside it — and comparability is the whole reason a network is worth more than the sum of its practices.

02What breaks at five hundred

Local marketing does not fail at scale. It fragments.

Nothing here is a criticism of how 123Dentist runs marketing. Every one of these is the arithmetic consequence of buying five hundred independent practices that each kept their own name, their own site and their own vendor.

One

Five hundred definitions of a new patient.

One agency counts form fills. Another counts phone calls, including the ones nobody answered. A third counts anyone who clicked. Roll those up and the network number is not wrong so much as meaningless — it cannot be defended line by line, which is the only way a number survives a board.

Two

The best clinic and the worst clinic look identical from head office.

Without a common measure you cannot tell a clinic with a marketing problem from a clinic with a front-desk problem, or from a clinic in a market that simply has fewer patients. So the budget conversation becomes a negotiation instead of a calculation, and the loudest region wins.

Three

Onboarding a clinic takes longer than buying it.

The deal closes. Then someone has to find the previous agency, recover the ad account, work out who owns the Business Profile, discover the tracking was never installed, and rebuild it — clinic by clinic, thirty-five times a year, while the campaigns either run unmanaged or stop.

Four

Vendor count grows faster than clinic count.

Each acquisition can add a contract, an invoice, a login and a quarterly call. None of it was procured by you. All of it eventually lands on your desk.

The fix is not a better agency. It is a standard unit — one package, built the same way every time, producing one number defined the same way at every location. That is the entire proposal.

04How a booked patient is measured

Three layers. Two are ours. One is yours.

Layer one
Ours.

A tracked number per clinic, inserted dynamically on your existing site. Which call came from which keyword, at what hour, in which city. Your site. Your brand. Our tracking.

Layer two
Ours.

The booking path on the site, tracked end to end — including where it is abandoned.

Layer three
Yours.

Whether the call became an appointment, and whether the patient showed. That lives in your practice management system, and it stays there.

We do not integrate with Dentrix, Tracker, Cleardent, or any other practice management system. No integration. No IT project. No patient data leaves the clinic.

We need six columns in a shared sheet — date, tracked number, answered, booked, appointment date, showed. We fill the left half automatically. The front desk fills three columns. Two minutes a week.

Where a clinic cannot complete the reconciliation, that clinic’s dashboard reads “qualified calls,” not “booked patients.” We would rather under-claim than be caught over-claiming.

Why it is built this way: the moment a marketing vendor asks for practice management access, the request stops being a marketing decision and becomes an IT, privacy and legal decision. That review takes months, and it is the reason most attribution projects inside DSOs are still unfinished. Six columns in a spreadsheet is not a compromise. It is the version that ships in fourteen days.

Said plainly

SSS has no dental case study at network scale. That is exactly why this document proposes six clinics and not five hundred, why the thresholds are published before launch rather than selected afterwards, and why the fee is cancelable monthly with no assets to recover. We are not asking 123Dentist to trust a claim. We are proposing the cheapest available test of one. If the number holds, the data is yours and it came from your clinics. If it does not, the cost is four months of a cancelable fee, a media budget you controlled the entire time, and a precise read on where the Google funnel breaks in your network — which is worth having either way.

The arithmetic, stated once

At the floor — eight booked new patients per clinic per month — six clinics produce 576 new patients in a year and five hundred produce roughly forty-eight thousand. We are not promising forty-eight thousand patients. We are showing the shape of the unit if it repeats. What one new patient is worth to 123Dentist is a number you have to the dollar and we do not. This document gives you the cost side and agrees the threshold in advance. The multiplication is yours.

08Where we fit

This replaces nothing you already have.

A proposal that quietly displaces an internal team, a website vendor or a call-coaching platform is a proposal that dies in a hallway. So here is the boundary, drawn explicitly.

We run
  • Google Search, Display and Video for each pilot clinic
  • The Business Profile for each pilot clinic — accuracy, categories, photos, review replies
  • Call tracking, conversion tracking and the per-clinic dashboard
  • Review capture at the front desk
We do not touch
  • Your brand system, your creative standards or your national campaigns
  • Your websites — we place one snippet and one number, nothing else changes
  • Your practice management system, and no patient data at all
  • Your internal marketing team’s remit — we report to it, not around it
On call intelligence

If 123Dentist runs Patient Prism or anything like it, keep it. Prism grades the conversation. We buy the conversation. Those are different jobs and the two datasets reconcile — you can audit our booked-patient number against Prism’s call outcomes and we would rather you did. Judge us on your instrument, not ours.

On incumbent agencies

Nothing needs to be cancelled to run this pilot. Select six clinics where the incumbent is underperforming, or six with no agency at all, or run parallel in a market and compare. We are not asking you to break a relationship to test a hypothesis.

10What comes after Google

You own five hundred clinics. Your patients have never heard of you.

Every clinic in the network kept its own name — 12th & 8th Dental, 404 Dental, 7 Oaks. That was the right call for the practices and it leaves 123Dentist with a structural problem no advertising budget solves: at the point of care, the network is invisible. Google ends at the front door. These are the layers that start there. Both are in build. Neither is being sold in this document, and neither is priced here — they are the reason a four-month pilot is worth starting as a relationship rather than a transaction.

Layer one · in market

The front desk pad, co-branded.

The NFC review pad is already in the package. The version built for a network carries both marks — the clinic’s name the patient knows, and 123Dentist beneath it. A patient taps it at the moment they are happiest, leaves a Google review that lowers the cost of the next patient in that city, and learns for the first time that the practice they trust belongs to something larger. One object, doing local SEO and network brand at the same time.

Layer two · in build

The waiting room, and the recall that never gets made.

A Wi-Fi sign-in screen that carries the network’s brand and collects, with consent, the contact details of every patient who connects. Branded chargers and hardware that put 123Dentist in the room rather than on a contract. And an AI recall agent that works the list every clinic has and no front desk has time for — the patients overdue for hygiene, the treatment plans accepted and never scheduled. That list is already inside your network. Nobody is calling it.

Nothing in this section is part of the pilot, part of the fee, or part of what is being asked for today. It is here so that the four-month question is answered with the full picture rather than half of it.

12Questions you will be asked

Answered here so you do not have to defend them from memory.

What does this replace?

Nothing. Not your team, not your national campaigns, not your website vendor, not your call intelligence platform, not an incumbent agency you would rather keep. It is a per-clinic execution layer that sits underneath what you already run.

Who owns the accounts and the data?

123Dentist, from day one and without exception. Ads accounts live in your manager account, Business Profiles stay yours, and every month of data exports as files on request. We hold access, never ownership.

Do you need access to our practice management system?

No. No integration, no IT project, no patient data. The reconciliation is six columns in a shared sheet and three of them are filled by the front desk, two minutes a week.

How do three people serve a network this size?

Because the build is a written specification rather than a bespoke project, and execution runs on fulfilment infrastructure already operating at multi-hundred-account scale under that specification. Strategy, thresholds and accountability stay with the named partners. Build time does not change with volume.

What happens when a clinic underperforms?

It appears by name on the roll-up, ranked last, with the reason on the same page — usually answer rate, occasionally a market with genuinely thinner demand. We tell you before you ask, and where a clinic cannot reconcile its bookings we report qualified calls instead and claim nothing further.

Why not just give this to an existing agency?

You can, and for one clinic it makes little difference. The proposal is not that SSS runs better campaigns. It is that five hundred clinics built to one specification produce one number you can compare, and an agency roster assembled through acquisition structurally cannot.

What is the real risk here?

That four months pass, the number lands above your threshold, and the exercise cost roughly four months of a cancelable per-clinic fee plus a media budget you controlled throughout. There is no term, no exit fee and no asset to recover. The residual value is a precise read on where your Google funnel breaks, which is useful whoever runs it next.

Why does this need to happen this quarter?

Because millions of newly covered adults are selecting a dentist right now, once, and because every month of delay is another set of acquired clinics onboarded onto measurement that will have to be redone. Neither clock waits for a budget cycle.
